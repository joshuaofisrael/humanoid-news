110 AgiBot A3 humanoids set a Guinness record for the most robots dancing at once

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 26 October 2026 · Last updated 26 October 2026

Short answer: On 24 October, 110 Expedition A3 humanoid robots made by Chinese company AgiBot danced together at the Jinan Yellow River International Convention and Exhibition Center in Shandong province, setting a Guinness World Records title for the most humanoid robots dancing simultaneously, according to the Global Times and Dazhong Daily. A Guinness adjudicator, Hou Ying, announced the count of 110 and presented the certificate on site, Dazhong Daily reported.

According to the Global Times, the robots performed synchronised routines and formation changes, including repositioning, jumps and flips, coordinated by AgiBot's LinkBots cluster-control platform and ultra-wideband positioning that the company says is accurate to the centimetre. Robrief reported that the routine lasted 5 minutes 43 seconds. AgiBot said the robots will next be used in cultural and tourism performances, public services, research and education, the Global Times reported.

The newspaper framed the record as part of China's push from single-robot demos toward large-scale deployment, citing a Ministry of Industry and Information Technology official who said Chinese companies produced more than 40,000 humanoid units in the first half of 2026. It also quoted Wang Peng of the Beijing Academy of Social Sciences, who said many applications remain at the pilot or early commercial stage. For background, see our report on China's 100,000-unit outlook for 2026.

Analysis

Opinion: A choreographed routine is a controlled setting, so the record says more about fleet coordination and positioning than about how well robots handle messy real-world tasks. That coordination still matters for any operator hoping to run dozens of robots in one space.
