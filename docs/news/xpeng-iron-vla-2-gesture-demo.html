XPENG shows IRON switching between handshakes, fist bumps and high fives

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 25 October 2026 · Last updated 25 October 2026

Short answer: XPENG has published a research-and-development demonstration in which its IRON humanoid robot keeps up as a person switches between a handshake, a fist bump and a high five without any spoken commands, according to Humanoids Daily and Humanoid Guide. XPENG attributes the behaviour to its VLA 2.0 system, which it says reassesses what the robot sees every 0.2 seconds.

In the video, as described by both outlets, a tester offers a handshake, closes it into a fist and then moves to an open palm, and IRON adjusts its response each time. When the tester moves a high five out of reach of the arm IRON is using, the robot answers with its other hand. XPENG says the upgraded model has stronger temporal understanding and behaviour prediction and runs on the robot itself, Humanoid Guide reported.

Both outlets note the limits of what was shown. According to Humanoids Daily, the footage is labelled as an R&D test version, and XPENG did not disclose success rates, how many attempts were filmed, or end-to-end response times; the 0.2-second figure describes how often the model updates, not how long a full response takes. XPENG CEO He Xiaopeng described the demo as a step toward everyday use, the outlet reported.

Humanoids Daily reports that XPENG is targeting mass production and first deliveries of IRON in the fourth quarter of 2026. Other recent humanoid demos have also drawn close scrutiny, such as Unitree's H2-X guardian video.

Analysis

Opinion: Reacting to a gesture that changes halfway through is a small but genuinely useful skill for robots meant to work around people. Without success rates or unedited trials, though, the clip shows what the system can do on a good take, not how reliably it does it.
