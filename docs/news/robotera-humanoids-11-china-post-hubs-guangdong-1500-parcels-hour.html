ROBOTERA humanoids now sort parcels at 11 China Post hubs, at more than 1,500 an hour

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Chinese humanoid maker ROBOTERA says its robots are now working across China Post's Guangdong network, at 11 postal hubs in eight cities: Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Dongguan, Jiangmen, Shantou, Shanwei, Jieyang and Heyuan. The company announced the rollout on 17 November and calls it the largest and most geographically spread deployment of embodied AI robots in logistics so far, a claim it does not back with comparison figures.

The project began as a pilot at the Guangzhou Postal District Center in March 2026. ROBOTERA says its robots average an induction rate of more than 1,500 parcels per hour, which it describes as comparable to experienced human workers. Induction is the step of placing parcels onto a sorting line. The company says the system rejects irregular items and adjusts its speed to cut jams and missed parcels, and uses ROBOTERA's own five-fingered hand.

ROBOTERA argues the robots help most at peak times, when parcel volumes can reach several times their normal level, and on night shifts. Guangdong is China's largest province by express parcel volume, the company notes. It says the same system has also been deployed in postal operations in East China.

Two days later, ROBOTERA said it had been invited to the APEC CEO Summit, held from 17 to 19 November, for the second year running. According to that release, its M7 humanoid ran live parcel-sorting demonstrations, and founder and chief executive Jianyu Chen gave a talk at APEC Finance Night titled "Putting Robots to Work at Night". The company says it now has deployments with China Post and SF Express, with robots operating in more than 10 logistics centres.

ROBOTERA has not published how many robots are in service, how long they run between charges in live shifts, or what China Post pays. Earlier this month the company opened orders for its XHAND 1 Pro-U robot hand, and our deployment tracker follows announced rollouts like this one.
