Abu Dhabi's Lunate plans a humanoid robot ETF on the Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 17 November 2026 · Last updated 17 November 2026

Lunate, the Abu Dhabi investment firm, plans to launch an exchange-traded fund focused on humanoid robots, The National reported on X on Tuesday morning. According to the newspaper's article, Lunate said in a statement that the Boreas VettaFi Humanoid Robots UCITS ETF is expected to list on the Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange on 8 December under the ticker ROBOTS.

The fund will track the VettaFi Humanoid Robot Select Markets Net Total Return Index and will initially hold 41 companies from Asia, the US and Europe, The National says. They span four parts of the humanoid value chain: semiconductors, robot parts, humanoid robot developers and software. The article does not name the holdings or give the fund's fees.

Sherif Salem, partner and head of public markets at Lunate, called it the firm's fifth thematic offering on the exchange. The Boreas range already includes ETFs on AI data, power and infrastructure, quantum computing and luxury, and is listed on the ADX and Germany's Xetra. Lunate runs the range, with product development and research support from Northwind, its consultant on thematic ETF strategy.

Northwind chief executive Geir Espeskog said the fund aims to give exposure to humanoid developers and the supply chain behind them "rather than try to pick a single winner". The article also cites projections that annual humanoid shipments could rise from 20,000 in 2025 to 10 million in 2035, figures it does not attribute to a named forecaster.

Analysis

Most of the best-known humanoid makers are still private, so a listed fund like this mostly buys suppliers and large technology groups rather than pure humanoid companies. The holdings list, once published, will show how much of the fund is really about humanoids.

Note: This is news reporting, not investment advice. Details come from Lunate's statement as reported by The National; the fund's prospectus and holdings had not been published when we checked.
