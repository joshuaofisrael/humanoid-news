One Chinese robot maker pulled out of CES after all 15 of its engineers were refused US visas

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

The engineers who make Chinese humanoids walk on stage may not make it to Las Vegas. "Sources: technical staff at several Chinese humanoid robot makers have been denied US visas ahead of CES, hampering their efforts to showcase their hardware," Techmeme posted on X on Thursday, citing Nikkei Asia.

Nikkei Asia reports that researchers and engineers at several Chinese humanoid makers, including Xpeng and Engine AI, have been refused US visas ahead of the show in early January. According to EastFrontier's summary of the report, staff with science and engineering backgrounds were turned down for B-1/B-2 business and tourism visas, while most marketing employees were approved.

The losses are uneven. At least one Chinese robot maker has withdrawn from CES after all 15 of its technical staff failed to get visas, citing "geopolitical complexities", and at another about half of the applicants were rejected, according to people who spoke to Nikkei. Xpeng and EngineAI did not respond to Nikkei's requests for comment. AgiBot and Unitree remain on the exhibitor list, and people close to both said they still plan to send staff from mainland China.

Chris Pereira, founder of consultancy iMpact, told Nikkei that about 40% of his clients planning to attend CES had been refused, mostly AI and robotics executives and technical staff. He also pointed to Chinese restrictions on travel by AI talent, and expects more companies to choose shows in Europe and Singapore instead.

The refusals come after two US moves on imported robots this year: the FCC added foreign-made "advanced robotic devices", including humanoids, to its Covered List in July, and a Section 232 proclamation in October found that robotics imports threaten national security, without imposing tariffs yet, EastFrontier reports.
