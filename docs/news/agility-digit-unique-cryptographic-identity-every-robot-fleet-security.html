Agility says no two Digit robots share an identity, so one hacked robot can’t take down a fleet

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

As humanoids move into warehouses, the question is not just what they can lift but who else can control them. "Every Digit robot is its own secured system, with a unique cryptographic identity baked into the hardware. No two share an identity," Agility Robotics posted on X on Thursday. "The reason: if something happens to one unit, it stays contained — not spread across the fleet."

How Agility describes its security

The post links to a talk by CTO Pras Velagapudi. A RoboBoom summary of that talk says each Digit has a unique cryptographic identity, encrypted storage and controlled device access, and that robot-to-cloud traffic is authenticated at both ends and encrypted. Velagapudi says this helps limit a problem to a single unit.

Two other points stand out. Safety limits are stored on each robot, so a lost connection, a remote command or outside access cannot override them. And because Digit's cameras may capture faces, shipping labels or customer products, the cloud side has to protect that data too. Velagapudi points to industry incidents where hidden access paths let attackers send commands or see data across many robots, without naming companies.

The labour pitch

Later on Thursday Agility shared comments from CEO Peggy Johnson to the Financial Times. Manufacturers face "an existential problem with the labor that they can't get," she said, according to the company's post, which adds that workers are retiring, fewer are replacing them and reshoring is adding demand.

Why it matters

Buyers are moving from pilots to fleets. Agility is now listed on Nasdaq, as covered in our AGLT debut story, and is adding sites with GXO, as covered in our GXO story. When a site runs dozens of robots, one compromised unit should not mean a fleet-wide incident. Security is becoming a selling point.
