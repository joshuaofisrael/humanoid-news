Joyson Electronics delivers its first robot brain backpack compute module to Unitree

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 12 November 2026 · Last updated 12 November 2026

Chinese auto parts maker Joyson Electronics said it delivered its first high-performance robot brain backpack to Unitree Robotics on 10 November, moving the two companies' strategic partnership into the product delivery phase. The announcement was distributed through PR Newswire on 11 November. Yicai reported that the unit was supplied by Joyson's Ningbo-based robotics subsidiary.

According to Joyson, the backpack combines an edge computing platform with an energy system and works with Unitree's humanoid, quadruped and wheeled robots. It can run as a standalone external compute module for perception, data collection, autonomous decision-making and motion control, and supports running embodied AI models on the robot itself. Joyson also says it improves energy management and significantly extends how long a robot can operate between charges. The company did not publish compute, power or battery figures.

The two companies are also jointly developing dexterous hands and an energy management system for high-performance solid-state batteries, Joyson said, with the goal of standardised components that can be mass-produced and used across different robot types.

Beyond components, the partnership targets industrial use: Joyson said the companies are working on data collection in automotive parts production, combined hardware and software, training industrial foundation models, pilot deployments on production lines and wider replication. Yicai said both sides expect further progress within the year.

Analysis

Bolting extra compute and battery onto a humanoid as a backpack is a practical way to run larger on-board models without redesigning the robot. Without published specifications, though, it is not yet possible to compare the unit with the compute already built into Unitree's robots or with modules from other suppliers.
