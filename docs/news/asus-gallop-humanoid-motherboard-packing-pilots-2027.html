ASUS unveils Gallop, a humanoid learning to pack motherboard accessories, with customer pilots planned for 2027

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 13 November 2026 · Last updated 13 November 2026

Taiwanese computer maker ASUS has introduced Gallop, its first humanoid robot, and says the machine is already learning a real job in one of its group factories. According to an ASUS pressroom post, Gallop is training at an electronics manufacturing services (EMS) plant to pack motherboard accessories, a station built for human workers. Customer pilots are planned for 2027, with market availability targeted for the second half of 2028.

ASUS chose a hard first task. The station involves 15 small parts, many of them in thin, clear bags, and takes a person about 20 seconds per board. The company says its target is 99.5% success within 20 seconds, the same standard it holds people to. Small, thin and soft parts are exactly what simple grippers struggle with, which is why ASUS wants two arms and dexterous hands at an unchanged human workstation.

The robot learns mostly by watching skilled workers. ASUS says experienced staff wear a small head camera, with their consent, so Gallop can see how the job is really done; simulation then generates variations of each example, and an operator wearing a VR headset and gloves guides the robot through the task on the same parts and cameras as the real station. ASUS says it does not build its own giant AI model but starts from leading open models and swaps in better ones as they arrive, thanks to a modular design. Interesting Engineering reported the same targets and timeline.

ASUS co-CEO S.Y. Hsu first introduced the robot in a CNBC interview, the company says. ASUS has not published a price, a production volume or test success rates to date, so the 99.5% figure is a goal rather than a result.

Analysis

ASUS is pitching an advantage most humanoid start-ups lack: its own factories to test in, on real parts and under real quality rules. A single packing station with a published success target is a narrower, more checkable claim than a general-purpose demo, and the 2027 pilots will show whether Gallop can hit it without a person standing beside it.
