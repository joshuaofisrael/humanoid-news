Unitree CEO says GPT-6 powered humanoids painted and played chess with no prior training

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 6 November 2026 · Last updated 6 November 2026

Short answer: Unitree Robotics founder and CEO Wang Xingxing said at the China International Import Expo in Shanghai that the company's humanoids, powered by OpenAI's GPT-6, can paint pictures and play chess without prior training, according to a South China Morning Post report published on 5 November 2026. The claims are Wang's, as carried by SCMP; this desk has not independently verified a public demo video of those exact tasks.

What Wang said, per SCMP

SCMP writes that Wang spoke on Thursday at CIIE and described Unitree machines executing intricate sequences such as dynamically replacing brush water and blending pigments, guided by realtime model inference. "The precision required to change pigments without error is quite high," Wang said, per the paper. "Seeing it perform at this level with zero training was truly stunning."

The same article says Wang argued the industry's biggest remaining hurdle is no longer digital reasoning or planning but closing a "last millimetre" mechanical gap. That framing treats model side planning as largely solved in his view, and hardware precision as the bottleneck.

How to attribute this

Treat every capability claim here as a CEO statement reported by SCMP, not as a third party lab result. SCMP does not, in the text reviewed, publish a controlled benchmark score or an outside auditor's confirmation. OpenAI's own GPT-6 Cyber materials are about security research agents and are a separate product thread; Wang's remarks concern Unitree robots said to run on GPT-6 for physical tasks.

Until Unitree or OpenAI publish reproducible demos with clear task definitions, the accurate headline is that Wang says GPT-6 powered Unitree humanoids painted and played chess with no prior training, as reported by SCMP from CIIE.
