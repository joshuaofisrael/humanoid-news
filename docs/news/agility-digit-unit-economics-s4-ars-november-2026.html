Agility's S-4 and Ars interview put Digit unit economics in the open: $8,500/mo RaaS, $200k buy, BOM path to $30k

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 5 November 2026 · Last updated 5 November 2026

Hours after Agility Robotics began trading as AGLT, Ars Technica published a detailed look at the company's S-4 registration statement and an interview with co-founder Jonathan Hurst that puts Digit 5's proposed unit economics in plain numbers. This desk already covered the Nasdaq debut itself; the Ars piece is useful for the pricing and cost path that the listing day press release largely skipped.

Short answer: According to the 4 September S-4 filed with the SEC, which Ars also reviewed, Agility's illustrative robots-as-a-service model charges about $25,000 one-time for delivery and deployment plus a recurring $8,500 a month for software and maintenance. Customers can instead buy the hardware for a one-time $200,000 plus about $20,000 deployment, then pay about $36,000 a year ($3,000 a month) for software and maintenance. Ars says Agility estimates its own annual software-and-maintenance cost at $15,000 per robot and a one-time $15,000 deployment cost to Agility.

On the manufacturing side, the S-4 and Ars put Digit v5's bill of materials at about $150,000 at commercial launch, with targets of about $75,000 at 1,000 robots a year and $30,000 at 10,000 a year, with a useful life of about five years. CFO Michael Beer, in an 6 October investor presentation summarised by Ars, framed subscription as replacing roughly $200,000 in annual labour with about $100,000 in annual robot cost, and ownership as replacing about $1.1 million in labour with about $400,000 in robot cost over five years. Those labour-replacement figures are Agility's illustrations, not audited customer results.

Ars also notes Agility earned $1.8 million in revenue against a $140 million operating loss in 2025, and quotes the S-4 warning that the company expects to keep losing money for the foreseeable future. The subscription revenue maths in the filing, Ars writes, lean on an unidentified customer's $300 million order for 1,000 Digit 5 robots over three years—the S-4 describes it as one customer's commitment to deploy $300 million of Digit v5 over a multi-year period, subject to contractual milestones, product features and specifications. Agility's listing-day release cites more than $300 million in committed multi-year Digit 5 orders on the same conditions.

Hurst told Ars that RaaS lowers customer risk because the monthly rate is "comparable to the human labor rate," and that Digit 5 is aimed at structured workplaces, not homes. Nyobolt's fast-charge pack is described as delivering 90 minutes of runtime from a nine-minute charge under an exclusive humanoid chemistry deal. Readers should treat battery, safety and labour-replacement claims as company statements relayed by Ars.

What this adds beyond the debut release is a public price book and cost curve that competitors still mostly keep private. Whether those numbers hold once Digit 5 ships in volume is the open question the S-4 itself flags.

Analysis

Opinion: Listing-day headlines sell the ticker; the S-4 numbers sell the hard part—subscription ARPU, BOM scale and years of losses. Treat the $8,500 and $30k BOM targets as planning assumptions until Agility reports repeatable deployments at those rates.

Note: Pricing, deployment and bill-of-materials figures are from Agility's S-4 filing as reported by Ars Technica; revenue, loss and labour-replacement figures are as reported by Ars from the S-4 and company presentations. This is news analysis, not investment advice.
