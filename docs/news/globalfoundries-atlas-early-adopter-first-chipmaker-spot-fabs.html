GlobalFoundries will be the first chipmaker to test Boston Dynamics' Atlas humanoid, starting in early 2027

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 18 November 2026 · Last updated 18 November 2026

GlobalFoundries (GF) will be the first semiconductor manufacturer to test Atlas, Boston Dynamics' electric humanoid, as part of the Atlas early adopter programme. Boston Dynamics announced the expanded deal on X on Tuesday, saying it was "thrilled to expand our collaboration" with the chipmaker.

According to the company's announcement, GF is the first customer outside the automotive industry to begin testing Atlas, and it calls this the first programme of its kind for semiconductor manufacturing. Testing on site at GF facilities is due to start in early 2027. The early adopter programme and a skills development workshop will be used to evaluate possible humanoid use cases, check how ready GF is to integrate the robots, and begin early testing and training on specific tasks.

The deal also widens GF's use of Spot, Boston Dynamics' four-legged inspection robot, to its fabs in Malta, New York; Burlington, Vermont; Dresden, Germany; and Singapore. GF has run Spot in Burlington for more than four years. In Singapore, Boston Dynamics says two Spot robots have travelled nearly 900 kilometres and completed more than 90,000 inspections in sub-fab areas since April, with a 99% mission success rate.

"Scaling Spot and testing Atlas are the next steps in our journey to AI-enabled manufacturing," said Pradip Singh, chief manufacturing officer at GlobalFoundries. Zack Jackowski, Boston Dynamics' chief product and technology officer, said companies like GF "are realizing real operational value today while collecting the kind of data that will be extremely useful toward future manufacturing innovation".

Yonhap describes the Atlas tests as the robot's first trial outside the auto industry. Boston Dynamics is owned by Hyundai Motor Group, whose car plants have been Atlas's main proving ground.

Analysis

Fabs are a hard test for a humanoid: tight cleanroom rules, expensive tools and little room for mistakes. The announcement describes evaluation and training, not production work, and gives no number of robots or tasks, so the first public results from GF will be the real signal.
