Figure 03 Robots Leave BotQ on Foot, but Production Scale Remains Unclear

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 3 November 2026, 20:01 UTC · Last updated 3 November 2026

Figure founder and chief executive Brett Adcock posted a roughly 19-second video on November 2 showing newly manufactured Figure 03 humanoids walking outside the company’s BotQ manufacturing facility. Several white-and-black robots move along a building approach and toward a marked pedestrian crossing, with people nearby. Adcock described them as heading across campus after leaving BotQ, according to Humanoids Daily.

The images are a useful snapshot of physical output, but they answer fewer business questions than their visual impact might suggest. The post gives no current weekly production rate, number of units completed in the latest batch, customer shipment total or breakdown between research and commercial use. Nor does the video establish whether the walking sequence was fully autonomous, supervised remotely or run under another control arrangement.

Those distinctions are especially important for a company positioning humanoids as a scalable workforce technology. A robot that walks from a factory building is a manufactured unit; it is not necessarily an installed, revenue-generating machine. Factory output, delivery, acceptance testing and paid deployment are separate milestones, and counting them interchangeably would inflate the commercial significance of a campus clip.

Figure has previously supplied more concrete markers. In April, Adcock said BotQ had produced more robots in March than over the company’s entire prior history, although he did not provide a unit count in that update. In July, the company celebrated production of its 1,000th Figure 03 with a gold-colored unit and a team photograph. That was a cumulative manufacturing milestone rather than a statement that 1,000 units had been delivered to buyers. The November footage adds evidence that machines continue to emerge from BotQ but no updated cumulative figure.

The reason manufacturing matters goes beyond sales. Figure develops its own robot-control AI, Helix, and a larger internal fleet can provide more opportunities to collect manipulation and navigation examples, test component durability, and expose failure modes that do not appear in a single demonstration. Even robots retained on campus can contribute to development, although the value of that data depends on how varied and representative their tasks are.

Figure’s reported commercial and pilot activity has included work with BMW Manufacturing and retail group Catalyst Brands. Those relationships provide possible environments for testing whether robots can handle the repetition, interruptions and safety requirements of real workplaces. But the November walking footage is not itself a new announcement about either customer. It does not identify the destinations of the robots shown.

The company also faces the problem of model turnover. While Figure 03 units are leaving BotQ, Figure is developing Figure 04, which could alter the economics of tooling, spare parts, servicing and customer adoption. Manufacturers often need to stabilize a design long enough to build it repeatedly, even while engineering teams prepare the next generation. A rapid succession of prototypes can impress observers without producing a predictable delivery schedule.

For prospective customers, the relevant follow-up disclosures would include completed robots per month, how many pass final acceptance tests, how many reach external sites, and what work they perform after installation. For investors, the same figures would help distinguish capital-intensive fleet building from recurring commercial demand.

The new video does demonstrate something concrete: multiple newly completed humanoids moving through an ordinary outdoor campus setting. It should not be promoted as a new sales record or evidence of mass deployment. Figure’s manufacturing story will become much easier to evaluate when the company pairs visible robots with comparable production and field-performance data.
