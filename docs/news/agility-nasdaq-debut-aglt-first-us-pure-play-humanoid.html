Agility begins Nasdaq trading as AGLT, first U.S. pure-play listed humanoid company

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 5 November 2026 · Last updated 5 November 2026

Agility Robotics began trading on the Nasdaq Stock Market under the ticker AGLT on 5 November 2026, describing itself as the first U.S. publicly listed pure-play humanoid robotics company with active commercial deployments. The company announced the debut on X: "Today, Agility begins trading on the Nasdaq Stock Market under the ticker symbol AGLT."

Short answer: According to Agility's press release, common stock and warrants trade as AGLT and AGLTW after completion of the previously announced business combination with Churchill Capital Corp XI. The business combination and related financings represent approximately $465 million in gross capital raised before transaction expenses, the company said. A companion release covers the closing of that combination.

Agility says it enters the public markets with nearly three years of real-world deployment experience, more than 65,000 hours of operation, and more than $300 million in committed multi-year orders for Digit 5, its next-generation humanoid, subject to contractual milestones, product features and specifications. Those order and hour figures are the company's own disclosures in the Nasdaq debut release, not independently audited totals published in the sources reviewed here.

Digit is already operating with enterprises including GXO, Schaeffler and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada, the release states. Agility recently expanded its partnership with GXO to deploy Digit at two additional U.S. logistics facilities in early 2027, building on a Georgia site where Digit has moved more than 100,000 totes, according to the same announcement. The company's growing customer base also includes AGCO under a commercial agreement described in the debut release. CEO Peggy Johnson said becoming public gives Agility access to more resources to accelerate workplace deployments of robots that take on repetitive, physically demanding tasks.

Digit 5 is presented as a cooperatively safe platform intended to work closer to people than earlier generations, supported by Agility's robotics stack, safety systems and the Agility Arc enterprise software platform. The release frames that software and safety layer as part of how Agility plans to scale deployments, not merely as a hardware refresh. Readers should treat product-roadmap language as forward-looking statements from the company.

The "first U.S. publicly listed pure-play humanoid" claim is Agility's characterisation of its listing. It does not mean Agility is the only company working on humanoids, nor that every peer is private; it means Agility presents AGLT as a pure-play humanoid listing with commercial Digit work already under way. Other robotics or automation firms may trade publicly with broader product mixes; Agility's point is the pure-play humanoid framing plus live customer sites.

Investors should read the full SEC and company filings for risks, dilution, warrant terms (AGLTW) and how much of the roughly $465 million gross capital remains after fees. A listing day is not a revenue forecast. Past tote counts and pilot hours do not assure that Digit 5 multi-year orders will convert on schedule or at the margins Agility needs.

What to watch next is whether Digit 5 orders convert on the stated milestones, whether the additional GXO sites open as described for early 2027, how Toyota and Schaeffler deployments expand, and whether Agility publishes clearer unit economics once it reports as a public company.

Analysis

Opinion: A Nasdaq ticker is a financing and visibility event, not proof that humanoid robots are ready for every warehouse. The useful evidence in Agility's own release remains the deployment hours, named customers and conditional Digit 5 orders—figures that still need to be tested against delivery schedules and margins over the next several quarters.

Note: Share prices, order totals and capital raised are as stated by Agility Robotics in its 5 November 2026 announcements. This is not investment advice. Past deployments do not assure future revenue.
