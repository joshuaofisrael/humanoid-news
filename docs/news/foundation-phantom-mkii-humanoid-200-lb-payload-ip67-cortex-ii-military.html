This humanoid carries 200 pounds through rain and snow, and its maker won't rule out arming it

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

Most humanoid makers want their robots in warehouses. Foundation wants its new one on building sites and battlefields. "US firm unveils Phantom MKII battlefield humanoid robot with bigger payload, tougher build," OpenEye Comms posted on X on Saturday. "Shaped by Ukraine field trials, Foundation's Phantom MKII targets combat logistics."

The spec sheet

Foundation's product page lists the MKII at 6.0ft tall and 195lbs, with a payload of "200lbs / 90 kg", 29 degrees of freedom and a runtime of 4 to 6 hours from a 3 kWh battery. It is rated "Weatherproof IP67" for "rain, dust, heat, snow", has modular joints that "swap out so the work doesn't stop", and is "designed in California, made in the USA", with scaling planned for 2027. The company says the specifications "describe shipping capability".

The robot ships with Cortex II, Foundation's control software, which the company says learns new tasks from human demonstrations. Interesting Engineering, citing a Fast Company interview, says some tasks need as few as 60 examples.

What changed from MK-1

Related reading

According to Interesting Engineering, the redesign fixes problems found in field trials of the first Phantom: battery runtime, waterproofing and lifting capacity. CEO Sankaet Pathak says simpler assembly halves the bill-of-materials cost. Foundation sent two MK-1 units to Ukraine earlier in 2026 for non-combat logistics and reconnaissance tests, the outlet says, though reports later disagreed over how much they were used.

The weapons question

Pathak told Fast Company that current defence work is logistics, not combat, but that Foundation would consider arming its robots if the US military asked, with a human keeping final authority over any use of force, Interesting Engineering reports. "I don't think you can build a utopia and then not defend it," he said.

Reasons for caution

Interesting Engineering also notes, citing CNBC and 36Kr, that the start-up recently faced cash-flow pressure, including a missed payroll in late October. The 200lb figure and the weatherproofing are Foundation's claims and have not been tested independently. The thing to watch is whether early production starts in the Bay Area in early 2027, as planned, and whether military research deals turn into orders.
