ROKAE says it has delivered its 10,000th humanoid force-controlled arm

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 26 October 2026 · Last updated 26 October 2026

Short answer: ROKAE Robotics, a robot maker listed in Hong Kong, said on 26 October that it has delivered its 10,000th humanoid force-controlled arm, according to a company press release. The company says the milestone unit came off the production line at its Shandong base in September, and that the count covers only its humanoid arm line.

According to the release, five ROKAE Helios wheeled humanoid robots fitted with its AR Series arms are working at Schaeffler's lighthouse factory in Taicang, China, and more than 80 industrial partners use its arms. ROKAE says it was founded in 2014 and has more than 1,000 customers in over 40 countries. Those figures are the company's own and have not been independently verified.

The release also describes ROKAE's compact MAC controller, which the company says measures 213 by 145.5 by 45 mm and can run 7 joints, 14 joints or more than 20. Founder and CEO Tuo Hua is quoted as saying, "There are no shortcuts in force control." Schaeffler's Taicang site is covered in our earlier report.

Analysis

Opinion: Component suppliers reaching five-figure volumes is a quieter but useful signal of where humanoid demand actually sits, though a delivery count says nothing about how those arms perform once installed.

Note: Delivery numbers, partner counts and deployment details are ROKAE's own statements and have not been independently verified. ROKAE is a listed company; this article is news, not investment advice.
