Robotera opens orders for the XHAND 1 Pro-U, the robot hand used in NVIDIA's GR00T 2 DexUMI demos

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 17 November 2026 · Last updated 17 November 2026

Beijing company Robotera has opened commercial orders for the XHAND 1 Pro-U, a five-finger robot hand designed to match NVIDIA's DexUMI exoskeleton, Humanoids Daily reports. It is the hand NVIDIA used in its DexUMI demos of the GR00T 2 robot model, shown at the Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL) in Austin last week.

DexUMI is a wearable hand. A person puts it on and handles real objects while it records finger joint angles and fingertip forces. The XHAND 1 Pro-U has 16 degrees of freedom laid out to match the exoskeleton, so motion captured on a human hand maps directly onto the robot hand, according to the report.

Two of the hands ran on the Tianji dual-arm robot in the demos, in which the robot made banana yogurt and lit a lamp with a match. Robotera reports success rates of 60%, 80% and 75% on three multi-step tasks trained without teleoperation. Those are the company's figures, not independent results.

The first batch is limited to 40 units. Robotera has not published a price, weight or grip force, Humanoids Daily says. NVIDIA has released the DexUMI exoskeleton design files under a CC BY 4.0 licence, and the GR00T 2 model weights are due in December.

Analysis

Hands are one of the hardest parts of a humanoid, and data collection is the bottleneck for training them. A commercial hand that matches an open exoskeleton design gives labs a ready-made pairing for gathering human demonstrations, which could matter more than the hand itself.
