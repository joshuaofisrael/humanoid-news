Andy Rubin's Genki Robotics shows its humanoid in public for the first time, to Japan's prime minister

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 14 November 2026 · Last updated 14 November 2026

Genki Robotics, the Tokyo humanoid start-up founded by Android creator Andy Rubin, has shown its robot in public for the first time. According to Humanoids Daily, the company brought it to a meeting with Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi at the Prime Minister's Office on 12 November, where Rubin, its chief executive, and chief technology officer Koichi Nishiwaki represented the firm.

The unit was very new. Genki said the last parts arrived the previous Friday, the team assembled it over the weekend and it took its first steps on Monday, Humanoids Daily reports. At the meeting the full-size biped stood on a fixed support rather than walking, while video beside it showed it walking and doing tabletop work, RobotToday says.

Genki told the meeting it has been through several rounds of prototypes and is building its first product model toward the end of the year. It listed target abilities as walking on flat ground and stairs, patrolling and carrying objects, and said it designs its own hardware, from joint actuators to cameras, drawing on Japanese component makers and supply chains. RobotToday reports the company is also talking to Japanese telecom operators about connecting the robot to mobile networks, with no agreement or timeline disclosed.

Humanoids Daily reports that Rubin co-founded the company in May 2025, that it raised a seed round of about $50 million, and that it was reported in April to have reached a valuation of about $1 billion in a Series A.

The meeting was part of the government's physical AI push. In remarks published by the Prime Minister's Office, Takaichi said her cabinet has made physical AI one of 17 strategic fields under the Japan Growth Strategy, with targets of about 10 million AI-powered robots deployed by 2040 and a 20 trillion yen market, more than 30 percent of the global total.

Analysis

Genki is pitching an Android-style open platform for humanoids while leaning on Japanese suppliers for hardware. A robot that first walked days before its debut is a prototype, not a product, so the year-end product model is the next checkable milestone.
