LG Electronics signs up to help write Korea's humanoid robot safety and quality tests

By Humanoid News Staff · Published 16 November 2026 · Last updated 16 November 2026

LG Electronics has signed a memorandum of understanding with the Korea Robot Industry Association to develop safety and quality evaluation standards for humanoid robots, The AI Insider reports. The agreement was signed on 13 November in Seoul.

The two sides will work on testing procedures, certification requirements and standards meant to judge how safe, reliable and usable humanoid robots are when they work alongside people. LG says it wants safety and quality testing to start earlier in development, before robots reach homes and workplaces.

Under the deal, LG will help set up the evaluation system for the association's Humanoid Robot Safety Certification Center and take part in developing national and international standards. The company also plans to pursue pilot safety certifications in South Korea for robots including its LG CLOiD.

The agreement comes as LG develops a bipedal robot based on NVIDIA reference technology, with an unveiling targeted for the first quarter of 2027, according to the report. LG has previously shown CLOiD learning manufacturing tasks at its LG Data Factory, which collects data used to train robots.

Kim Chang-min, head of LG's Quality Management Center, said the work would help LG become a robot brand customers can use with confidence. Association president Cho Young-hoon said trust in safety and quality is essential for humanoids to spread into daily life and industry.

Analysis

Shared test methods matter more than any single robot launch if humanoids are to work near people. Being in the room when the rules are written also gives LG an early read on what its own 2027 robot will need to pass.
