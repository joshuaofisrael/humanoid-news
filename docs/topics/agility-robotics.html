Agility Robotics news: Digit, Digit 5 and AGLT listing

By Humanoid News Staff

Agility Robotics and its Digit humanoid: who uses it, GXO and AGCO deployments, Digit 5 timing and orders, the Churchill XI merger and AGLT Nasdaq listing, and every story, each linked to its sources.

10 stories · last updated 20 November 2026

Latest in this topic

What we know so far

As of 31 October 2026 for the deployment figures below; listing status updated 20 November 2026, based on the stories below and their cited sources.

  • Agility announced on 5 November 2026 that it had completed its business combination with Churchill Capital Corp XI and that its common stock had begun trading on Nasdaq as AGLT. The company said the combination and related financings represented about $465 million in gross capital before expenses. That is the company's account, in its 5 November release (our listing report). The October announcement of the expected close is still linked from our earlier AGLT report. This is not investment advice.
  • Agility says Digit already works with GXO, Schaeffler and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada, and that it has added four customers in automotive, consumer goods and agriculture (Digit 5 and Pittsburgh report).
  • GXO, Agility's first commercial customer, has used Digit at Flowery Branch, Georgia, under a multi-year agreement that began in June 2024, and Agility says Digit has moved more than 100,000 totes there. GXO plans two more US sites, Statesville, North Carolina, and Lebanon, Tennessee, from early 2027 (GXO expansion report).
  • AGCO plans a Digit pilot at its Hesston, Kansas, factory from 2027, starting with sorting and stacking parts and placing totes for storage (AGCO pilot report).
  • Digit 5, the next version, will run on NVIDIA IGX Thor onboard computing. Agility says early-access deliveries should begin in the first half of 2027, and its 30 October announcement targets general availability for the end of 2027.
  • Agility says the Digit 5 battery, supplied by Nyobolt, will run for 90 minutes and recharge in 9 minutes, which it says lets the robot work more than 20 hours in a 24-hour day across repeated charges (Agility, 15 October; runtime comparison).
  • Agility says it holds more than $300 million in committed multi-year Digit 5 orders, subject to contractual milestones and without naming the customers. It describes the figure as potential multi-year value, not current revenue.
  • Three more manufacturers, none named, are in paid programs, and Agility says it has a pipeline of more than 30 prospective customers across manufacturing, automotive, aerospace, defense, logistics and consumer products.

Questions readers ask

What does the Digit humanoid robot do?

In the deployments Agility has announced, Digit handles repetitive material handling: moving totes in GXO's e-commerce fulfilment sites, and, in AGCO's planned pilot, sorting and stacking parts and placing totes for storage.

When will Digit 5 be available?

Agility says early-access deliveries of Digit 5 should begin in the first half of 2027, and its 30 October 2026 announcement targets general availability for the end of 2027. These are the company's targets.

Is Agility Robotics a public company?

Agility announced on 5 November 2026 that its common stock had begun trading on Nasdaq as AGLT after it completed the combination with Churchill Capital Corp XI. That is the company's account of the listing. This is not investment advice.

What is the AGLT ticker?

AGLT is the Nasdaq ticker Agility announced for its common stock when trading began on 5 November 2026. The company said the combination and related financings were about $465 million in gross capital before expenses. Warrants trade as AGLTW, according to the same release. This is not investment advice.

How long does Digit 5's battery last?

Agility says Digit 5's Nyobolt battery will run for 90 minutes and recharge in 9 minutes. These are the company's own figures, not an independent test.

How many Digit robots are deployed?

Agility has not published a robot count in the announcements we have covered, and GXO's expansion did not say how many robots will go to the new sites.
