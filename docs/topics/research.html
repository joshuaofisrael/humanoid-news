Humanoid robot research and AI models

HumanoidBeat coverage of humanoid robot research: control methods, robot foundation models and new training approaches, as described by their authors. Newest first; each story links to its sources.

8 stories · last updated 26 October 2026

  • ETH Zürich's ACP policy lets a Unitree G1 climb ladders, vault and get back up after falls
    26 October 2026 Researchers at ETH Zürich's Robotic Systems Lab report a single learned policy, Attention-based Contact Planning, that plans where a humanoid's hands, knees, feet and pelvis should touch the terrain, letting a Unitree G1 climb, vault and recover from falls.
  • Amazon-led team teaches a humanoid to hold boxes against its torso and under its arm
    26 October 2026 A preprint from Amazon, UC San Diego and the University of Michigan describes Whole-Body Dexterity, a controller that lets a teleoperated Unitree G1 brace objects against its arms and torso, completing 45 of 50 real-world trials.
  • Figure says Figure 04 is in design lock and teases Helix 3.0 in a public all-hands
    25 October 2026 In a published all-hands recording, Figure CEO Brett Adcock said Figure 04 has reached design lock, its Index app has 37.5 million human videos, and previewed Helix 3.0.
  • Alibaba's Amap team releases λ0, a humanoid model trained largely on human video
    25 October 2026 Researchers at Alibaba's Amap and Token Hub released λ0, a whole-body humanoid model trained on a 500-hour human video dataset; they report 62.5% success on four real-world tasks with a Unitree G1.
  • XPENG shows IRON switching between handshakes, fist bumps and high fives
    25 October 2026 A new XPENG video shows its IRON humanoid adapting as a tester changes gestures mid-movement; XPENG credits its VLA 2.0 model, which it says re-evaluates the scene every 0.2 seconds.
  • Why UCLA's PhoneBot, a small humanoid run by a smartphone, is getting attention
    24 October 2026 UCLA's open-source PhoneBot uses an Android phone for sensing and computing in a 48 cm biped that its researchers say costs about $400 without the phone.
  • Unitree says it is building "Rehearsal", a self-improving loop for robot software
    22 October 2026 Unitree's Wang Xingxing said on 22 Oct 2026 the firm is developing "Rehearsal", a loop for robots to write code, test it in simulation and improve (SCMP).
  • Berkeley's SuperMimic trains a humanoid motion controller in three hours on one GPU, authors report
    21 October 2026 UC Berkeley researchers report that SuperMimic matched a baseline humanoid motion tracker that trained for seven days on 128 GPUs, using three hours on one GPU.
